## Pillars

1. **Indirect intervention.** The player is a god helping a much weaker army survive a much stronger attacker. Powers act on the world (weather, water, terrain, vegetation), never on soldiers. Examples: rain that overflows rivers to slow the attacker; lightning that fells a tree across a path to redirect it. Consequences can kill, eventually: an overflowing river, a bridge that collapses under too much weight, ice that breaks under a crowd, a herd stampeding from a fire, a wind-driven fire front, boulders a tremor sets rolling. Soldiers can shape the world too (digging, filling, burning, herding; settled 2026-10-09), but only specialist unit types, each through its own fixed battle rule (settled 2026-10-10), never as the god's hand.
2. **Range rule (100 m).** No power can be cast on or reach either army directly: its whole area must be at least 100 m from both armies at the moment it fires, or it fizzles. Since armies move, the player must act ahead of them: a game of anticipation and setup. Terrain pieces follow the same 100 m, measured from the deployment zones.
3. **Massive asymmetric armies.** Up to 10,000 units; types: infantry, cavalry, ranged, siege.
4. **Physics-based.** Outcomes come from simulation, not scripted effects.
5. **Shader-heavy.** Visuals ideally are the gameplay state.
6. **Windows PC.** Mouse and keyboard, large screen, Windows only at launch. Runs on integrated graphics at 20 fps or better; a dedicated GPU is recommended (settled 2026-10-11).
7. **Three ways to play, three layers.** A puzzle-like singleplayer campaign where you defend as the god; a singleplayer attack mode where you lead the strong army against an AI god who adapts round by round (settled 2026-10-11); and asymmetric PvP (attacker versus defender) as an auto-battler. All run on the same simulation and are planned in the same three layers: position the army, shape the terrain, use god powers. In every mode the battle itself takes no input: both armies are AI-driven, and the player's whole influence is the plan.
8. **Timeline casting.** Powers are queued over time; a forward-running deterministic simulation shows the future and can be moved forward and back.
9. **Powers counter powers.** Fire, wind, rain, cold and the rest counter each other through the shared world fields.

## Campaign structure

Each level is planned in three phases, the same layers as PvP:

1. **Phase 1:** position the army (within the level's deployment zone).
2. **Phase 2:** shape terrain (pieces placed before the battle that exist from the first tick: dig or raise ground, add water, grow trees, add swamp, set boulders, place herds, and built pieces such as bridges, walls, towers, hedgerows and dams), at least 100 m from every deployment zone.
3. **Phase 3:** use god powers (timed events on the timeline), each at least 100 m from both armies when it fires.
- The level sets each phase's constraints (the army and its deployment zone, the terrain pieces available, the powers in hand); those constraints are the puzzle.
- Early levels open the phases one at a time (for example powers only with a pre-placed army, then terrain, then all three), so each layer is taught on its own.

Working assumption until confirmed: a level is one editable plan. The phases set the order of planning, but the player can go back to any phase at any time, and the battle preview runs from phase 1 onwards. No adjust phase is needed, since everything stays editable; changing an earlier phase re-validates the later casts on the timeline.

## Attack mode structure

Singleplayer, settled 2026-10-11: the player takes the attacker's seat against an AI god who defends. The AI god prepares terrain and god powers and changes them between rounds in response to what it has seen; the player buys, places and upgrades units to break through.

Working assumptions until confirmed:

- Same match structure as PvP: up to five rounds, each replaying the battle from the start with bigger budgets; placements carry over; a wipe of the defending army in any round wins.
- The AI god plays fair: it adapts only to what it has seen (earlier rounds' battles and carried-over units), never to the player's new, still-hidden purchases; its own new placements stay hidden until the battle starts, then are revealed, as in PvP.
- The AI god has a budget and a readable personality (for example a storm god, a fire god, a builder), so its adaptations can be learned and played around; difficulty comes from its budget growth and personality, not from extra information.
- Units are upgraded with bought levels, not earned veterancy (from `claude/unit-brainstorm.md`: drill and kit per regiment, hidden like new placements), with the same economy as PvP.
- The player's seat matches PvP's attacker: many bodies and a small energy pool for a few powers, so attack mode also trains the PvP attacker.

## PvP structure

Asymmetric, and modelled on auto-battlers such as Mechabellum. One player attacks, the other defends:

| Seat | Gets |
|---|---|
| Attacker | Many bodies (the strong army), little god-power energy |
| Defender | Few bodies (the weak army), much god-power energy |

- **One match, fixed seats.** Each match has one attacker and one defender, and the seats don't swap.
- **Five attempts, one wipe wins.** The attacker has five rounds. Wiping out the defending army in any one of them wins the match; the defender must keep part of its army alive through all five.
- **Every attempt is a reset with a bigger budget.** Each round the battle replays from the start: both armies come back at full strength, and losses never carry over. Both seats get more resources to spend each round, and everything bought or applied (units, terrain pieces, queued powers) carries over. Nothing is commanded or cast by hand during a battle.
- **Removal costs.** A carried-over placement stays where and when it was set. Either seat can remove one of its own, but removing costs resources rather than refunding them.
- Each player sees the other's earlier placements; new ones stay hidden until the battle starts. Every failed attempt therefore shows the attacker more of the defence, and the defender must keep finding fresh surprises.
- **Two budgets, both limited, both growing (settled 2026-10-11).** Bodies buy the army layer (units and their upgrades); energy buys terrain shaping and god powers. Each seat gets an income of both every round, larger each round; nothing can be bought beyond what the seat has. The attacker's income is mostly bodies, the defender's mostly energy. The same rules bind the AI god in attack mode.

Working assumptions until confirmed:

- Both seats plan in all three layers from round 1, each limited by its budget (a power-less round 1 would hand the attacker the match).
- Unspent budget doesn't carry over to the next round, so neither seat can bank a round's income for one decisive round (the 2026-10-10 review's risk).
- A queued power is paid once: it stays on the timeline and fires again, at the same time and place, in every later round until it is removed (at a cost), like any other placement.
- The defender holds a round if any of its army is still alive at the round's time limit (sunset), or if the attacking army routs.
- Stragglers don't count: a regiment reduced below about 10% of its starting size counts as wiped, as the range rule already ignores stragglers, so one soldier hiding in a corner can't hold a round.
- Units that flee off the map count as wiped; routed units still on the field don't, so the attacker must ride them down.
- No direct moving or re-timing: to move a placement or change a cast's time, pay to remove it, then buy it again.

## Open questions

- PvP budgets: how much does each seat's budget grow per round (the main balance knob, since the attacker also learns every round)? How much does removing a placement cost: a flat fee, or a share of its price?
- PvP rounds: how long is a round (the sunset limit)? With a wipe required, round length decides whether a wipe is reachable at all, so it is a balance knob as strong as budget growth.
- PvP seats: how are seats assigned (each player queues for a seat, random, or alternating between matches), and is rating kept per seat? (Recommended: a separate rating for each seat, plus an optional rematch with seats swapped.)
- Terrain buffer: does the 100 m rule also bind pieces a level designer pre-places in the campaign? (Working assumption: no, it binds the player only.)
- Towers and walls: can the attacker capture and use them (recommended: yes, so they are double-edged)? Can a dam's crest be used as a crossing? (Breaching is settled: units can shape the world, so sappers, firebrands and siege can breach built pieces.)
- World-shaping specialists: do the most world-changing actions, fire above all, also cost energy, or bodies only? (Working assumption: bodies only, with a set number of charges per battle.) (The first set of specialists is settled, 2026-10-11, below; Firebrands, Sappers and Wheel-Breakers follow once fire and towers exist.)
- Deployment: is it position and facing only, or can the player also set a standing stance per regiment before the battle (hold, skirmish, counterattack when an opening appears)? A stance chosen in planning would still leave the battle itself pure AI.
- Campaign army: does the player choose its composition (a budget, as in PvP), or only position a fixed army the level provides?
- Campaign phases: can the player return to earlier phases freely, or does a phase lock once finished? (Working assumption: free.)
- Counters in the campaign: does the attacker have its own priests or god casting powers, or do counters there only answer map hazards (drought, forest fires the attacker sets)? (In PvP the attacker has a small energy pool. Since units can shape the world, attacker units such as firebrands, fascine carriers and sappers already give the campaign attacker counters without a god.)
- Timeline: casts pinned to clock times only (recommended to start), or also to events ("when the vanguard reaches the ford")?
- Is the god's foresight limited (how far ahead the preview reaches)?
- Campaign: does the attacker follow fixed, learnable behaviour, or adapt to the player?
- How immediate may a harmful consequence be? (The 100 m rule already stops a felled tree landing on a column; a stampede can still arrive within about ten seconds.)
- Do morale or omen powers count as reaching the army directly?
- Wind reach: a wind power's area stays 100 m from the armies, so does the wind it creates keep blowing downwind past that area, reaching the archers? (Recommended: yes, for a set distance; otherwise wind can't affect arrows.)
- Wind: does each map have a prevailing wind the level sets (which wind powers then bend), or is the air still until a god casts wind? Does a crosswind push arrows sideways, or does only the headwind or tailwind part count? (Recommended: only the along-shot part.)
- Ice: does a frozen stretch stop the river's flow (so ice backs water up like a dam), or does water keep flowing beneath it?
- Attack mode format: PvP's five-round match (working assumption), or a longer run across several maps where the army and its upgrades carry over (like IB 30, The Long Retreat, from the attacker's side)?
- Attack mode powers: does the player get the PvP attacker's small energy pool for a few casts (working assumption), or is it units only?
- Cavalry in deep mud: when a charge hits deep mud, do the horses stop dead and throw their riders forward (a launch, mostly stun)? Would make rain the cavalry counter in one funny beat. (From the staple funny situations in `claude/power-palette.md`.)
- Slipping on ice: do running and charging units slip, spin and stun on ice, as a rule separate from the load check?
- Animals in hazards: when herds are caught by water, ice or fire, do the animals die like soldiers, or always paddle or run off unharmed? It sets the tone of every stampede gag.
- Death remains: do pancakes, soot-black figures and floating gear vanish after a few seconds (the playful-tone rule, 2026-10-09, and the working assumption), or stay on the field for the whole battle as visual-only marks?
- Art direction: units match TABS-level detail with their own look, and the tone is playful (settled 2026-10-09), but which look: painted tabletop miniatures, carved and painted wooden figures, tin soldiers, something else?
- Camera and map scale (the 100 m terrain buffer around both deployment zones sets a floor on map size)
- Energy amounts: how much each seat gets per round and how fast it grows (with the body budgets, the main balance knobs). Does the campaign give each level a fixed energy budget? (Working assumption: yes, set by the level, with no rounds.) No cooldowns or charges are planned on top of the budget.
- PvP planning time: is there a timer per planning round? (Working assumption: yes in PvP, about 60 to 90 seconds; none in the campaign or attack mode.)
- Campaign win and loss conditions

## Decisions

- **2026-10-08 — Platform: desktop PC.** Mouse and keyboard controls; design casting, UI and performance budgets for desktop. *Hardware floor set on 2026-10-11: integrated graphics at 20 fps, laptops included (below).*
- **2026-10-08 — Powers can kill through consequences.** A power never strikes soldiers, but the world it changes can kill them later (an overflowing river sweeps troops away, a bridge collapses under too much weight). Harm is delayed and indirect, from world state the army enters or triggers.
- **2026-10-08 — Modes: puzzle-like singleplayer campaign, plus PvP (god versus god).** Consequence: the simulation must be deterministic (reproducible puzzles, identical battles on both players' machines), built in from the first prototype. *PvP format superseded on 2026-10-09: attacker versus defender (below).*
- **2026-10-08 — Timeline casting with a scrubbable future.** Powers are queued at times on a timeline; the deterministic simulation runs forward and the player can move it forward and back. The range rule is checked at each cast's execution time against the simulated armies. Consequence: the simulation must run roughly 10 to 20 times faster than real time, with keyframe snapshots for scrubbing.
- **2026-10-08 — PvP is an auto-battler, modelled on Mechabellum.** Placements persist into later rounds, and new ones are added each round; the battle plays automatically. A round ends when one army is wiped; the losing god takes damage equal to the winner's remaining units. *Damage rule superseded on 2026-10-09: five attempts, one breach wins (below).*
- **2026-10-08 — Powers counter other powers.** Counters work through the shared fields (wetness, heat, wind, temperature, water), so they are partial, positional and timed rather than outright cancels. Turnabouts (turning a setup against its owner) are preferred. Counter web: `claude/power-palette.md`.
- **2026-10-08 — PvP layered rounds.** Round 1: place armies. Round 2: shape terrain. Round 3: use god powers. Round 4: adjust. Gods build their own armies; terrain shaping is a separate, static layer placed before the battle, distinct from timed god powers. *Superseded on 2026-10-09: with one breach enough to win, a power-less round 1 would hand the attacker the match, so both seats plan all layers every round (working assumption).*
- **2026-10-08 — Campaign uses the same three phases as PvP.** Phase 1: position the army. Phase 2: shape terrain. Phase 3: use god powers. Consequence: both modes share one plan format (army placements, terrain edits, timed casts) and one planning interface, and the campaign teaches the layers PvP is played in.
- **2026-10-08 — Wind affects arrows.** Arrows shot into a strong wind fly slower and fall shorter; shots downwind carry further. Wind is world state both armies' archers fly through, so wind powers stay indirect while becoming the main lever on ranged combat.
- **2026-10-08 — Cold freezes water.** Below freezing, water turns into ice over time (thin first, thickening while the cold lasts). Ice is a load-bearing surface: light units cross, heavy units break through, and a frozen river is a crossing for both armies.
- **2026-10-08 — Fire melts ice.** Heat from fire turns ice back into water. Thinning ice loses strength, so fire beside a frozen crossing can drop an army through the ice it is on.
- **2026-10-08 — Weak ice breaks under crowds.** Ice holds a load set by its thickness. When too many units stand on a stretch at once, it breaks and they fall into the water. This scales with mass: a thin file crosses ice that a dense column breaks, and heavy units count for more.
- **2026-10-08 — Terrain shaping toolkit.** Terrain shaping (campaign phase 2, the PvP terrain layer) can dig or raise ground, add water, grow trees, add swamp, set boulders and place herds that move and block paths during the battle. Clarification of "static" in the layered rounds decision: terrain pieces are placed before the battle and never timed, but some then act on their own (water flows, herds roam).
- **2026-10-08 — Herds can be stampeded into armies.** Fire, lightning or another fear source spooks a placed herd into a stampede, which can run into an army. The god never aims the herd: it flees away from the fear source, steered by terrain, so the player sets it up through where the herd grazes and where the fire starts. Stampedes break formations, trample and spread panic, and don't pick sides, so they can hit the defender too.
- **2026-10-08 — Terrain pieces stay 100 m from deployment zones.** Every terrain piece must be placed at least 100 m from both armies' deployment zones. Working assumptions: measured from the edge of the zone (not from where troops stand, so adjusting regiments never invalidates terrain) to the nearest edge of the piece's whole footprint (a lake's shore, a ridge's slope, a herd's grazing area); pieces may still act into the buffer during the battle (floodwater flows in, a herd wanders or stampedes), like any other consequence.
- **2026-10-08 — God powers use the same 100 m range rule.** A power's whole area must be at least 100 m from both armies at the tick it fires; otherwise it fizzles, and the timeline flags it. One distance for every power, matching the terrain buffer. Working assumptions: measured to regiments' formation outlines, ignoring isolated stragglers, so a lone scout can't block casting across the map; consequences may still travel into the buffer (water flows, fire spreads, smoke and fog drift, herds run).
- **2026-10-08 — Terrain can include built pieces.** Terrain shaping isn't limited to nature: the god can also place built pieces such as walls, hedgerows, dams, herd pens and bridges, under the same rules as natural pieces (placed before the battle, 100 m from deployment zones). Working assumptions: built pieces block, funnel, hold water or carry load, but never harm soldiers by themselves (no stakes or pits; towers fight only through their garrison, see below); their material sets their counter (wood and hedges burn and rot, stone falls only to a tremor or flood), so none is a permanent lock. Seeds: `claude/power-palette.md`.
- **2026-10-08 — Walls and towers are allowed.** Fortifications join the built pieces: archers in a tower gain height and range, infantry behind a wall gain cover from arrows. Working assumptions until confirmed: they obey the 100 m buffer, so the defender must march out of its deployment zone to man them before the attacker arrives; wooden ones burn, stone ones fall to a tremor or siege engines.
- **2026-10-08 — Towers and walls fight only through their garrisons.** A tower or wall never shoots or harms soldiers by itself; it lifts and shelters the regiment inside it. Harm always comes from soldiers or from the world's consequences, never from a piece the god placed. Consequence: an empty tower does nothing, so getting a regiment into it in time is part of the plan, and the right garrison (archers in towers, infantry on walls) matters as much as where the piece stands.
- **2026-10-08 — The defender AI chooses garrisons.** The player never assigns regiments to towers or walls; the defender AI decides who mans each one. The god steers it only indirectly, through where regiments are deployed (campaign phase 1, the PvP army layer) and where the structures stand, which fits the indirect pillar. Working assumptions: the AI follows fixed, readable rules (suitable type first, archers for towers and infantry for walls, then a regiment that can arrive before the attacker, then the nearest), assigns once at battle start and re-assigns only if a garrison is lost, so the choice is deterministic, learnable and stable under small edits; the timeline preview shows which regiment heads for each structure, when it arrives, and flags structures left unmanned.
- **2026-10-08 — The defender is pure AI during the battle.** In both modes the player gives no commands once the battle runs; the defender AI fights on its own, and the player shapes it only through deployment, terrain and powers. Consequences: the battle takes no input in either mode, so the plan is the whole game and the preview is the main interface; the defender AI's quality becomes load-bearing, since it must spot and exploit the openings the player creates (counterattack a split column, hold a ridge, fall back behind a flood) and show in the preview which openings it has noticed, or the player's setups feel wasted.
- **2026-10-09 — PvP is asymmetric: attacker versus defender.** One player commands the strong attacking army, the other defends with the weak army. The attacker gets more bodies and less god-power energy; the defender gets fewer bodies and more energy, so one side wins through mass and the other through the world. Supersedes god versus god. Consequences: PvP is the campaign's premise with a human in both seats, and the armies are unequal by design; the attacker still has a little energy, so powers still counter powers in PvP, and his few casts are best spent answering or turning the defender's setups; the battle still takes no input from either seat (working assumption), which keeps the preview and the deterministic replay intact.
- **2026-10-09 — PvP match: five attempts, one breach wins.** The attacker has five rounds; beating the defending army in any one of them wins the match, and the defender must hold all five. Replaces the god-health damage rule. Consequences: a match lasts at most five rounds; every failed attempt reveals more of the defence, so the attacker probes and learns while the defender must keep adding fresh surprises; one bad round loses the defender the match, so the attacker can gamble and the defender cannot; the layered rounds no longer work, since a power-less round 1 would hand the attacker the match.
- **2026-10-09 — PvP rounds reset; budgets grow; purchases carry over.** Each attempt replays from the start with both armies at full strength, so the attacker's losses never carry over. Both seats get more resources to spend every round, and everything bought or applied (units, terrain pieces, queued powers) carries over. Consequences: each round is a clean, readable battle, and the strategy lives in what each side builds across the match; with no attrition the defender holds by surviving each round, so delay is as valid as killing, and lethal setups pay off only within the round; the plan grows every round, so round 5 is the largest battle to simulate and to read, and how fast each budget grows is the main balance knob against the attacker's growing knowledge.
- **2026-10-09 — PvP placements can be removed, at a cost.** Carried-over placements stay where and when they were set; either seat may remove its own, paying resources rather than getting a refund. Consequences: commitment is real but never permanent, and removal is the release valve for round 5 clutter; a known setup still shapes the opponent's route, so paying to remove one only makes sense when it blocks your new plan or now helps the other side; the removal price is a second balance knob next to budget growth, since too cheap turns persistence into rebuilding every round and too dear leaves dead weight.
- **2026-10-09 — No placement may fully trap either army.** Terrain and built pieces, alone or combined, must always leave each army a way out. A placement that would close the last route is refused and flagged, like a fizzling cast. Working assumptions: only hard blockers count (walls, boulders, cliffs, deep water, dense forest; mud, swamp, shallow water and snow are slow but open); the route must exist for every unit type, so wide units such as siege get wide enough gaps; "a way out" means a route from each deployment zone to the other army and to its own retreat edge of the map, so the defender can't wall the attacker out and hold every round by default; in PvP, if both seats' hidden pieces together close the last route at lock-in, a fixed rule opens a gap; blockers created during the battle (floods, fallen trees, broken ice, drifts) are exempt but must clear on their own or be clearable. Consequences: funnels and chokepoints stay legal and become the main job of walls; a full ring of walls needs a gate; the campaign can't be solved by walling the attacker in. Build: `claude/tech-notes.md`, section 3.
- **2026-10-09 — The guaranteed route has a maximum length.** The shortest open route between the two armies may be at most twice the straight-line distance between their deployment zones, for every unit type; a placement that would push it past that is refused, like one that closes the route. Working assumptions: the cap counts distance only, not travel time, so slowing the attacker with mud, swamp, rain or snow stays legal (that is the god's job); the same cap applies to each army's retreat route; maps are authored so their own shortest route sits well under the cap; the multiplier is a tuning knob that may differ per map. Consequence: a maze can't stall the attacker until sunset, so the defender holds by making the route dangerous or slow, not by making it long.
- **2026-10-09 — PvP rounds are won by wiping out the defender.** The attacker wins a round, and with it the match, only by wiping out the defending army; anything short of that by sunset is a hold. Settles the open question on what counts as beating the defender. Working assumptions: regiments reduced below about 10% of their starting size count as wiped, and units that flee off the map count as wiped, while routed units still on the field don't. Consequences: the defender's goal is survival, not victory, so falling back, splitting off a reserve and buying time are all strong plays, and the defender AI must value keeping regiments alive; the attacker needs both a breach and a hunt, so cavalry that can ride down fleeing regiments gains value; round length now decides whether a wipe is reachable at all; a winning round ends the moment the wipe completes, while holding rounds run to sunset.
- **2026-10-09 — A PvP match is a single leg with fixed seats.** One player attacks and the other defends for the whole match; seats never swap. Settles the two-legs question. Consequences: a match stays short (at most five rounds); seat balance can't come from structure, so it rests on tuning (budget growth, removal cost, sunset length), aiming for both seats to win about equally often on each map; players may prefer one seat, so queue design and per-seat ratings matter.
- **2026-10-09 — Units match TABS's level of detail, with their own look.** Soldiers are as detailed as in Totally Accurate Battle Simulator (simple, chunky, flat-coloured bodies; gear that reads from the camera; motion that looks physical), without copying its look. Working assumptions: the wobble is faked on the GPU (springs driven by the simulation's knockback, crowd pressure and the ground fields), with real ragdolls only for deaths near the camera and visual only, so outcomes stay deterministic and 10,000 units stay affordable; motion damps with distance so regiments still read as shapes. Consequences: the low detail per soldier suits the 10,000-unit budget; physical motion becomes an up-close warning layer (slipping in mud, flailing on cracking ice, wading as water rises); the look and the tone are still open. Spec: `claude/unit-spec.md`. *Wobble and ragdolls superseded on 2026-10-09: exaggerated physics, no wobble (below).*
- **2026-10-09 — Exaggerated physics, no wobble; performance first.** Physical consequences are exaggerated: soldiers hit by cavalry charges, stampedes, falling trees, collapses and siege stones are thrown much further, and tumble more, than reality would allow. Soldiers don't wobble (no idle sway or spring motion), and performance takes priority over any visual flourish. Working assumptions: the exaggeration lives in the deterministic simulation, not only on screen: a launched soldier becomes a ballistic body (fixed-point position, velocity and spin, with reduced gravity for hang time) and lands where the simulation says, so what the player sees is what happened; launch distance and damage are tuned separately, so most launched soldiers survive stunned and out of formation, and landing in water, off a cliff or into fire is what kills (shape first, kill second); a landing knocks neighbours down but never launches them, so there are no chain reactions; no runtime ragdolls, only the simulated arc with a baked tumble and crumple; animation clips still switch with the ground (slipping in mud, arms out on ice, wading), which costs nothing and keeps the warnings. Consequences: soldiers need no engine physics at all, so thousands can be airborne at once for a few numbers each; the playback track records launch events, so scrubbing and replays redraw identical arcs; charges and stampedes scatter regiments visibly at strategic zoom and feed morale shock. Spec: `claude/unit-spec.md`; build: `claude/tech-notes.md`, sections 5 and 10.
- **2026-10-09 — Playful tone.** The game reads as playful. The comedy comes from big physical consequences (a stampede flinging soldiers, a column windmilling on the ice before it breaks, a bridge tipping a regiment into the river), never from mocking the player or from luck. Settles the tone half of the art-direction question. Working assumptions: no gore (no blood, dismemberment or lingering bodies; the fallen tumble, splash, bob away and vanish like toys); drowning and crowd crush stay in the game but read as slapstick, never as suffering; the strategy stays serious, with warnings, preview and rules as precise as in a grave game, so humour lives only in presentation (animation, effects, sound, a few reaction barks, comic timing) and never in randomness that changes outcomes; a highlight camera that cuts to the deciding moment in slow motion is worth building (from the 2026-10-09 review; made configurable, below). Consequences: presentation targets the TABS and Bad North audience as well as strategy players; bodies never stay on the field, so they never feed back into pathing; every gag must leave the warning it rides on readable, since a joke that hides cracking ice costs the player a fair loss.
- **2026-10-09 — Configurable highlight camera; clips can be saved.** The highlight camera is a setting, not a fixed behaviour, and highlights can be saved as clips. Working assumptions: modes are off, an end-of-battle reel (the default, so the camera never takes the view away mid-battle), live cuts during the battle, or a picture-in-picture window, with slow motion as its own toggle; the reel shows the cause, then the payoff (the lightning, the herd breaking, then the peak of the flight); a clip saves in two forms, a video file captioned with the consequence chain ("lightning → oak → herd → 212 in the river") and a tiny replay file (map, plan and simulation version) that re-renders the battle from any camera; in PvP each seat can save its reel, and the loser's reel reveals the hidden pieces in the order they mattered. Consequences: the highlight picker reads the cause and launch events the simulation already records; sharing a replay costs kilobytes; replay files only play in a build with the same simulation version, so saving a clip should also offer the video. - **2026-10-09 — The preview shows where soldiers will land.** While planning, the player can see where launched soldiers will come down, red where the landing kills.
